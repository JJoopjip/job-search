# Grading rubric (agreed 2026-11-05)

The grade answers one question: **how likely is this to get an interview?** Judged against master.yaml facts only.

1. **Skills match (main driver).** Compare the posting's stated requirements with what master.yaml honestly supports.
   Requirements seen in a real description count; requirements assumed from a title are labelled "[assumed]".
2. **Level fit, penalised both ways, more for too senior.**
   - Manager or above, or "5+ years" asks beyond the candidate: lose about two notches.
   - Clearly below the candidate (clerical/admin support, assistant, technician): lose about one notch (overqualified screening, low fit).
   - Coordinator / associate / specialist / mid-level PM or BD: no penalty.
3. **Domain.** Health / pharma / medtech / health-tech adds about one notch. A strong non-health BD/PM match can still reach A.
4. **Title-only jobs (LinkedIn/Indeed alerts with no description).** Graded from title, company and location with no cap.
   They carry a "title only" tag on the card so the weaker evidence is visible.
5. **Eligibility.**
   - Hard blocker (French essential, US-resident only, clearance/citizenship required, role outside the markets): the job is
     hidden from the main list and appears under the "Excluded" tab, with the reason.
   - Unclear: keep the grade, show a "check eligibility" flag.
6. **Reality check after a resume run.** The resume generator's keyword coverage % and confidence appear on the card next to the grade.
   A low % is a nudge, not a verdict: much of it is posting boilerplate. A "thin" confidence means few of your bullets match.

Each graded entry carries `why`: {level, skills, domain} (one short line each) so a grade is traceable.
Grade scale: A strong match, B good, C partial/stretch, D weak, F off-target.
Verdict: apply = B- or better and no blocker; stretch = C+ to C; skip = below, or blocked.
